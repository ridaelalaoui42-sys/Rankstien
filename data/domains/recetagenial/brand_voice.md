# Brand Voice Guide: Receta Genial

**Mission:** Bring the soul of Spanish home cooking — paellas, tapas, guisos, and mariscos — to every kitchen, with recipes that taste like Sunday at abuela's but work on a Tuesday night.

---

## 1. Brand Personality: The "Genial" Spirit
- **Generous, not fancy.** Big-pan, big-table cooking. We celebrate sharing plates, not plating tweezers.
- **Confident in tradition.** When a recipe is regional (paella valenciana, pisto manchego), we say so and explain why it matters.
- **Practical.** Real timing, real substitutions for ingredients you can actually find.

## 2. Tone of Voice
- **Direct.** Active verbs. Short sentences. No SEO filler.
- **Warm.** Use second person, conversational. "Calienta el aceite" not "Se calienta el aceite".
- **Trustworthy.** When a step is the make-or-break, call it out: *"Aquí está la clave: …"*

## 3. Vocabulary
- Prefer Castilian Spanish terms (sartén, fogón, sofrito) but acknowledge LATAM equivalents in parentheses on first use.
- Common phrases: *"a fuego medio"*, *"hasta dorar"*, *"al punto"*, *"con su jugo"*.
- Avoid: foodie jargon, anglicisms unless required (*air fryer*).

## 4. Recipe Anatomy (every post)
1. 2–3 sentence hook — why this dish, why now.
2. Ingredients list (metric grams + cups in parens).
3. Numbered steps, ≤8 if possible.
4. **Truco del chef** — one short tip that prevents the most common failure.
5. Variations (1–2) — vegetarian / quick / regional.

## 5. Don't
- Don't use Isabella Dolce's persona — that's recetadolce. Use a generic "Receta Genial" voice or your house chef.
- Don't write desserts here as primary content — defer to recetadolce.com via internal-link policy.
